# 0023: An agent's system instructions stay the same; per-call lines, meeting turns and long-session meetings send less

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Related: [0005](0005-meeting-stage.md), [0010](0010-meetings-take-turns.md), [0011](0011-meetings-pause-for-research.md) (meetings, revised here), [0013](0013-agents-keep-role-notes.md) (role notes, revised here), [0017](0017-goal-mode-research-and-meeting-first.md) (a goal's research and meetings run once, revised here), `server/orchestrator.js` (`#system`, `#roleNotes`, `#notesFor`, `#runAgent`, `#meeting`, `#meetingPrompt`, `#prompt`, `#talkList`, `#longTalk`, `#meetPicks`, `#settle`, `notTalk`, `MEET_PICKS`, `HEAD_AGAIN`, `convSent`, `ctx.meetSteer`), `server/notes.js` (`notesKey`), `server/providers/claude.js` and `server/providers/codex.js` (no notes handling on resume any more), `server/providers/mock.js` (`prepTurn`), `public/js/views/session.js` (the "New conversation" chip), `test/prompt-assembly.test.js`, `test/helpers.js` (`promptBody`, `HEAD_STARTS`), `test/fake-cli/fake-claude.js`, `test/measure/prompt-audit.js` and `test/measure/prompt-compare.js` (the measurement)

## Context

A measurement with the test CLIs recorded the prompt and the system instructions of every call in five scenes (a three-stage team and a second request in the same session; a meeting in a fresh session; a full goal run with research, a meeting, task cards and one return; a session with 34 earlier messages that then holds a meeting; an agent with skills, role notes and project memory). It counts characters, not tokens.

- The same agent got different system instructions in different new conversations of one goal run: the research agent 3 different texts over 7 new conversations, the analyst 2 over 7, the writer 2 over 3. Three things changed them: the permission sentence (research turn, read-only turn, the agent's own), the "how to ask the user" lines (on in work stages, off in one-off turns), and the role notes' reference block, whose random marker is new on every call.
- That matters for two reasons. Prompt caching matches the start of a request exactly, and a change in the system prompt invalidates everything after it ([Claude Code, "How Claude Code uses prompt caching"](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/prompt-caching)); requests share a cache only when model and prefix match. And a resumed Claude conversation keeps the system prompt recorded on its first request until it is compacted: "every later request uses that recorded prompt", also after `--resume` ([Claude Code CLI reference, "System prompt flags in resumed conversations"](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cli-reference#system-prompt-flags-in-resumed-conversations); both pages read on 2026-11-25). So the per-call lines never reached a resumed Claude conversation, and decision 0013's "Claude gets the instructions on every call" was wrong: changed role notes never reached it either.
- A meeting turn that continued its participant's conversation sent again what that conversation already held: 9,176 of the 18,180 characters its turns, verdict and conclusion sent in the fresh-session meeting (lines of 20 characters or more that were already in that conversation).
- The research agent's two default skills are 20,686 characters of system instructions, and they went into every meeting opinion, turn, verdict and conclusion of that agent, which answer in text only.
- A normal meeting in the long session processed 282,071 characters (sent plus the participants' conversations read again) against 100,276 for the same meeting in a fresh session. Only a goal's meetings ran in new conversations (0017).

## Options

1. Keep the prompts as they are. Nothing to change, but the cache keeps breaking and resumed Claude conversations miss changed notes.
2. Fix the system instructions per agent and move the per-call lines into the prompt. For the skills of meeting turns, two ways were compared on the measured calls: (A) move skill bodies out of the system instructions into a fixed block at the front of the prompt and leave the block out of meeting turns; (B) a second fixed system instruction without skill bodies for meeting talk turns. Under a simple cache model (Claude calls only: a new conversation reads its system instructions from the cache when the same text came earlier in the scene; the first message of a new conversation is never shared between conversations; a continued conversation reads its earlier text from the cache), B wrote 136,393 characters fresh and read 98,192 back in the goal run, against 219,146 and 15,444 for A (latest measurement): with A every new work conversation sends the skill bodies as new text, with B they are read back. B was chosen. Either way, skill bodies can only be left out of a turn that runs in a new conversation of its own: a turn that opens or continues the participant's session conversation fixes that conversation's instructions (Claude keeps the first request's, Codex gets them only once), and later work in that conversation would lose the skills.
3. For meetings in a long session: (a) run every normal meeting in new conversations, (b) only in a long session, keeping continuing turns (with only new text) in shorter sessions, where reading back a short conversation is cheaper than sending everything again, (c) leave them. (b) was chosen.

## Decision

1. **System instructions per agent** (`#system`). They hold the name, role, the agent's instructions, the work folder, the sentence for the agent's saved permission (`savedPermission` when a call runs read-only), the review rule for a review role, the closing and language lines, the role notes' tasks, the user's items and the "n more items" line, and the skill bodies. Meeting opinions, turns, verdicts and conclusions that run in a new conversation get the same text without skill bodies (item 4). Everything that changes per call is returned as `head`: the read-only sentence of a goal's research or meeting call (0017), the line telling a read-only agent with tools to read CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md first (not for meeting talk turns that run in a new conversation, as in 0017), the "how to ask the user" lines when the run may ask, and the role notes' reference block with its random marker. `#runAgent` puts the head, with one blank line, at the front of the first prompt of a new conversation. The words are unchanged except the read-only sentence, which now adds `This line takes precedence over the saved permission in your instructions.`, because the instructions above it carry the saved permission's sentence. An AI whose read-only state is held only by words (`caps.permissions` false: Hermes, OpenAI-compatible APIs, models on this PC) is the exception: for its read-only calls the read-only sentence replaces the saved permission's sentence in the system instructions, as before this decision, and is not in the head, so it never gets "you can create and change files" and "don't change files" in one call; only those AIs give up fixed instructions for such calls. Within one session the remaining source of variation is the role notes' item pick when the items are over 2,000 characters (0013).
2. **A continued conversation gets only what it hasn't had** (`#runAgent` `start`, `#notesFor`, `convSent`). Claude keeps its first request's instructions and Codex got them once in its first message, so a continued call gets, in front of its prompt and in the head's place:
   - **Changed notes, as notes only.** "Changed" means the user's notes changed: `noteHashes` holds a hash of the tasks and every confirmed item the receiver can get (`notesKey`: id, source, address and text, by id), not of the items picked for one request. When it differs, the prompt starts with `Your role notes have changed. Follow the ones below instead of the role notes you got earlier. The rest of your instructions stay the same.` and a `<Role notes>` tag holding the tasks, the user's items, the "n more items" line and the reference block, or `Don't use the role notes you got earlier anymore.` when there are no notes now. The name line, the instructions and the skill bodies are not sent again, for Claude and Codex alike; nothing in the code needed Codex to get them again (0013 already said a change to the instructions or skills alone does not reach a resumed Codex conversation). The providers no longer add anything on resume.
   - **Long notes, the new picks only.** When the notes did not change but are over 2,000 characters, so a request with other words picks other items, the call gets only the items this request picked that the conversation has not had yet, after `Here are more role-note items related to this task. Follow them together with the role notes you got earlier.` Which items a conversation has had is kept in `convSent` (with that conversation's id; set from the picks of its first call and of a notes change).
   - **Missing head lines.** `convSent` also keeps which of the CLAUDE.md line and the "how to ask" lines the conversation got. A call that needs one the conversation didn't get gets it: for example a conversation opened by a meeting turn, which may not ask, and continued later by work that may ask. Every `HEAD_AGAIN` (10th) continued call since they were last added gets both again (478 characters in Korean when both apply: 52 for the CLAUDE.md line, 425 for the "how to ask" lines). This is the cheap part of the answer to compaction: the CLI reference says the recorded system prompt is used until the conversation is compacted, so after that it presumably uses the system instructions passed with that request (not checked with a real CLI), which no longer hold these lines, and the first message's head may be summarised away. The app does not notice compaction (`claude.js` reads only the `init` and `api_retry` system events; nothing else in the code mentions it), so the lines come back within ten calls instead of at once, and the reference block is not repeated (up to 2,000 characters; it comes back with the next notes change). `--system-prompt-snapshot off` was not used: it was never run with a real CLI.
   - A record whose conversation id is not the one being continued counts as unknown: the head lines are added and the picked items given again. A one-off turn that continues its own conversation inside the same turn (an extra instruction in a long session's meeting, item 5) gets nothing added; it got the head moments before. 0013 is revised.
3. **A continuing meeting turn gets only what is new** (`ctx.meetSeen`, `#meetingPrompt` `heard`). For each participant whose own conversation already holds a turn of this meeting, the meeting remembers how many of the "said so far" entries that conversation has received. Its next turn, verdict or conclusion gets the entries after that, without its own words, grouped by round. A turn in a new conversation (a goal's meeting, a first turn, a takeover by another AI, a failed resume, an AI that can't resume, a long session's meeting) gets everything as before; in every meeting its own earlier words now go apart under "[What you said earlier]" with the goal meeting's marker sentence that says they are inside the same markers, as goal meetings already did, instead of among "material other participants wrote". When nothing is new, the line explaining the markers is left out.
4. **No skill bodies in meeting talk turns that run in a new conversation** (option B above): every opinion, turn, verdict and conclusion of a goal's meetings and of a long session's normal meetings (item 5). Research during a meeting keeps them. A normal meeting in a shorter session keeps them too, because its turns open or continue the participant's session conversation, which later work continues (see Options). Each of the two system instructions is fixed per agent. Talk turns in a new conversation also don't get the CLAUDE.md line, as in 0017.
5. **Meetings in a long session run once per turn** (`#longTalk`, `ctx.meetFresh`, `once.fresh`, `msg.freshMeet`). Outside goal mode, when the session's earlier conversation that can be handed on (`#talkList`, the same list `#fullTalk` uses) has more messages than `historyTurns` × 2 (16 by default; with `historyTurns` 0 the default 8 is used for this check, so a session with a few earlier messages doesn't count as long), every turn of the meeting, research included, runs once in a new conversation: no conversation id is read or saved. Each turn gets the request in full, the earlier stage results as now, what was said in the meeting, and the "picked parts of the earlier conversation" block (`#meetPicks`, `MEET_PICKS`): first the 4 most recent messages before the request (up to 1,000 characters each), then, with what is left of 5,000 characters, `pickEarlier` over the rest (the first two messages and those that share words with the request, 600 characters each). For comparison, an agent taking over a session gets up to 32,000 characters of recent messages, 4,000 of picked parts (20,000 for an AI without tools) and the whole-conversation file. There is no "[Previous conversation]" block, no whole-conversation file, no handoff note, no project memory and no missed conversation. Extra instructions are taken as before; one that can't be injected live goes into the same turn's conversation afterwards, and that call's Claude usage is counted against the cumulative of the turn's earlier call (`#settle` with the turn's own `call.bases`; before, it counted only the main agent's part and no cost). An extra instruction sent to a turn (live or afterwards) is also kept per participant (`ctx.meetSteer`) and goes into that participant's later turns of the meeting as `[Additional user instructions]`, the place for the user's words, since each turn is a new conversation; in a shorter session the participant's conversation keeps it. The turns are marked `once` and `freshMeet`; unlike a goal's meeting turns they are handed on to later conversations like other answers. The next stage gets the conclusion as before. With `historyTurns` 0 no earlier messages are handed on, as that setting means. The "New conversation" chip of such a turn says it is a meeting in a session with a long earlier conversation.

This adds `freshMeet` to messages and `convSent` (`{ [agentId]: { id, head, calls, items } }`) to sessions, and changes what the `noteHashes` values hash. Older versions ignore `freshMeet` and would leave such a meeting's turns out of handed-on conversations, as they do for a goal's meeting turns; they ignore `convSent`. Hashes saved before this change don't match, so each continued conversation with notes gets its notes block once after the update (the "no notes" hash is unchanged), and each continued conversation without a `convSent` record gets its head lines once.

## Measurement

The same scenes, the same script and the same counting before this change (the parent commit) and after it (test CLIs with answer lengths padded to realistic sizes). To measure again: `node test/measure/prompt-audit.js <out> --pad` in this repository and `node test/measure/prompt-audit.js <out-before> --pad --repo <a checkout of the earlier commit>`, then `node test/measure/prompt-compare.js <out-before> <out>`. The script uses `startTestApp` and the test CLIs only, and writes the research agent's two default skills as filler of the measured lengths unless `--skills <folder>` is given; the earlier commit measured with filler skills came within 7 characters per scene of the same commit measured with the real skill files. Characters, not tokens: a Korean character is often about one token and English about four characters per token, so the two don't scale together. "Sent" is what the app sends (system instructions of a new conversation plus the prompt). "Read again" is the earlier text of a continued CLI conversation that the call reads again. "Variants" adds up, per agent, the different system instructions its new conversations got.

- The team scenes did not get smaller: the per-call lines moved but are still sent (3 characters more in all, one blank line per call). Their gain is that each agent's system instructions are now the same text on every call, which the cache can reuse; how much that saves in real use wasn't measured.
- In the goal run the 6 variants are one per agent plus the research agent's second, skill-free instructions for its meeting turns, by design; before, the research agent had 3 and the analyst 2 inside the same kind of call. The skill bodies sent in new conversations went from 144,809 to 103,435 characters. The read-only sentence's added clause costs 356 characters over the run's read-only calls.
- In the fresh-session meeting the gain is the turns that no longer repeat what their conversation holds (8,318 characters fewer sent); the research agent there still gets its skills (item 4).
- In the long-session meeting nothing is read again: each meeting turn got 2,759 characters of earlier messages (the 4 most recent and the first 2 of 38), where before the continuing turns read back 19,314 to 42,633 characters of their own conversation (which had started with the recent conversation, picked parts and the file path). With only the first messages and the word matches (the earlier version of this change, without the recent messages) a turn got 703 characters and the scene sent 50,342.
- The session of 17 requests sends 481 characters more: the tenth continued call gets the CLAUDE.md line and the "how to ask" lines again (item 2), and every later call of that conversation reads them back (2,896 characters more read again over the 17 requests, 481 more for the request after them).
- Under the cache model the goal run's characters written fresh went from 176,817 to 136,393 (−22.9%) and the long-session meeting's from 71,134 to 52,943 (−25.6%). A model, not cache numbers from a real CLI.
- The scenes not shown (an agent with skills, role notes and project memory, its first and second request) moved by at most 1 character sent and 5 read again. In earlier runs of the same scene one prompt had or lacked the "nothing found in project memory" line depending on when the memory had indexed an earlier answer, not because of this change. None of the scenes changes role notes or has notes over 2,000 characters; that case is in `test/prompt-assembly.test.js` (four requests with other words to one conversation: the instructions are sent once).

## Not done

- Giving skills by name and path only, and letting the CLI read them, is left for later: whether a CLI reads a skill it is only pointed at was not checked with a real CLI.
- Writing the research note to a file and sending a path and a summary, sending only the latest earlier stage result in full, fewer recent messages (`historyTurns`), a light mode for one-off calls, not sending again what a continued card conversation already holds, and a usage screen by kind of call are left for later.
- Real cache hits were not measured: the numbers are characters from the test CLIs, and the cache model above is a model.
- Noticing compaction (for example from an event the CLI may print when it compacts) and sending the head again at once is left for later: no such event was seen from a real CLI, and nothing in the code reads one.

## Consequences

- Risk: lines that were system instructions are now in the user's message: the read-only sentence of a goal's research and meeting calls, the CLAUDE.md line, the "how to ask" lines and the role notes' reference block. A model may weigh them less. What a read-only call can do is still held by the CLI flags (0017), and the reference block was reference material already. A read-only call now sees the sentence for the agent's saved permission in its instructions and "this turn is read-only, and this line takes precedence" in its message; an AI held only by words keeps the read-only sentence in its instructions instead.
- Risk: meeting participants in a long session know less of the session than before (the 4 most recent messages and picked parts only; no file, handoff note or memory). A meeting that depends on something said in the middle of a long session can miss it. The threshold is a count of messages, so one more message can switch a session to this behaviour.
- Risk: a continuing turn relies on its CLI conversation still holding the earlier turns. A compacted conversation may only hold a summary of them; they are not sent again.
- A notes change sends a continued conversation the notes block once (tasks and up to 2,000 characters of items), for Claude and Codex; the instructions and skills are not sent again. The old notes stay in the conversation; the lines only tell the AI to use the new ones.
- Risk: after a Claude conversation is compacted it presumably switches to the current, fixed instructions (read from the CLI reference, not checked with a real CLI), and the head of its first message (the "how to ask" lines, the CLAUDE.md line, the reference block) may be summarised away. The app doesn't notice compaction; the first two come back within ten continued calls (item 2), the reference block only with the next notes change.
- Tests that look at the body of prompts strip the head with `promptBody` (`test/helpers.js`); `test/prompt-assembly.test.js` checks the head itself, and that a continued call whose notes didn't change starts with none of `HEAD_STARTS` and without `<role instructions>`. The demo provider and the test Claude find the research headings by line, since the head can come first.
- To undo: put the `head` lines back into the `#system` text and stop prepending them in `#runAgent`; drop the `talk` argument for skills; pass the whole instructions again on a notes change (`refreshSystem` in the providers, as before) and hash the picked block again; pass `null` as `heard` in `#prompt`; set `ctx.meetFresh` to false. `freshMeet` on saved messages and `convSent` on sessions can stay.
