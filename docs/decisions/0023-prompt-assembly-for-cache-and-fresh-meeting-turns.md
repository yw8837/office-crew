# 0023: An agent's system instructions stay the same; per-call lines, meeting turns and long-session meetings send less

- Status: Accepted
- Date: 2026-11-25
- Related: [0005](0005-meeting-stage.md), [0010](0010-meetings-take-turns.md), [0011](0011-meetings-pause-for-research.md) (meetings, revised here), [0013](0013-agents-keep-role-notes.md) (role notes, revised here), [0017](0017-goal-mode-research-and-meeting-first.md) (a goal's research and meetings run once, revised here), `server/orchestrator.js` (`#system`, `#roleNotes`, `#runAgent`, `#meeting`, `#meetingPrompt`, `#prompt`, `#talkList`, `#longTalk`, `#meetPicks`, `notTalk`, `MEET_PICKS`), `server/providers/claude.js` (`refreshSystem`), `server/providers/mock.js` (`prepTurn`), `test/prompt-assembly.test.js`, `test/helpers.js` (`promptBody`), `test/fake-cli/fake-claude.js`

## Context

A measurement with the test CLIs recorded the prompt and the system instructions of every call in five scenes (a three-stage team and a second request in the same session; a meeting in a fresh session; a full goal run with research, a meeting, task cards and one return; a session with 34 earlier messages that then holds a meeting; an agent with skills, role notes and project memory). It counts characters, not tokens.

- The same agent got different system instructions in different new conversations of one goal run: the research agent 3 different texts over 7 new conversations, the analyst 2 over 7, the writer 2 over 3. Three things changed them: the permission sentence (research turn, read-only turn, the agent's own), the "how to ask the user" lines (on in work stages, off in one-off turns), and the role notes' reference block, whose random marker is new on every call.
- That matters for two reasons. Prompt caching matches the start of a request exactly, and a change in the system prompt invalidates everything after it ([Claude Code, "How Claude Code uses prompt caching"](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/prompt-caching)); requests share a cache only when model and prefix match. And a resumed Claude conversation keeps the system prompt recorded on its first request until it is compacted: "every later request uses that recorded prompt", also after `--resume` ([Claude Code CLI reference, "System prompt flags in resumed conversations"](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/cli-reference#system-prompt-flags-in-resumed-conversations); both pages read on 2026-11-25). So the per-call lines never reached a resumed Claude conversation, and decision 0013's "Claude gets the instructions on every call" was wrong: changed role notes never reached it either.
- A meeting turn that continued its participant's conversation sent again what that conversation already held: 9,176 of the 18,180 characters its turns, verdict and conclusion sent in the fresh-session meeting (lines of 20 characters or more that were already in that conversation).
- The research agent's two default skills are 20,686 characters of system instructions, and they went into every meeting opinion, turn, verdict and conclusion of that agent, which answer in text only.
- A normal meeting in the long session processed 282,071 characters (sent plus the participants' conversations read again) against 100,276 for the same meeting in a fresh session. Only a goal's meetings ran in new conversations (0017).

## Options

1. Keep the prompts as they are. Nothing to change, but the cache keeps breaking and resumed Claude conversations miss changed notes.
2. Fix the system instructions per agent and move the per-call lines into the prompt. For the skills of meeting turns, two ways were compared on the measured calls: (A) move skill bodies out of the system instructions into a fixed block at the front of the prompt and leave the block out of meeting turns; (B) a second fixed system instruction without skill bodies for meeting talk turns. Under a simple cache model (Claude calls only: a new conversation reads its system instructions from the cache when the same text came earlier in the scene; the first message of a new conversation is never shared between conversations; a continued conversation reads its earlier text from the cache), B wrote 136,041 characters fresh and read 98,188 back in the goal run, against 218,790 and 15,444 for A: with A every new work conversation sends the skill bodies as new text, with B they are read back. B was chosen. Either way, skill bodies can only be left out of a turn that runs in a new conversation of its own: a turn that opens or continues the participant's session conversation fixes that conversation's instructions (Claude keeps the first request's, Codex gets them only once), and later work in that conversation would lose the skills.
3. For meetings in a long session: (a) run every normal meeting in new conversations, (b) only in a long session, keeping continuing turns (with only new text) in shorter sessions, where reading back a short conversation is cheaper than sending everything again, (c) leave them. (b) was chosen.

## Decision

1. **System instructions per agent** (`#system`). They hold the name, role, the agent's instructions, the work folder, the sentence for the agent's saved permission (`savedPermission` when a call runs read-only), the review rule for a review role, the closing and language lines, the role notes' tasks, the user's items and the "n more items" line, and the skill bodies. Meeting opinions, turns, verdicts and conclusions that run in a new conversation get the same text without skill bodies (item 4). Everything that changes per call is returned as `head`: the read-only sentence of a goal's research or meeting call (0017), the line telling a read-only agent with tools to read CLAUDE.md or AGENTS.md first (not for meeting talk turns that run in a new conversation, as in 0017), the "how to ask the user" lines when the run may ask, and the role notes' reference block with its random marker. `#runAgent` puts the head, with one blank line, at the front of the first prompt of a new conversation and of a call that re-sends the instructions after a notes change; a continuing conversation already has it. The words are unchanged; only the place moves. Within one session the remaining source of variation is the role notes' item pick when the items are over 2,000 characters (0013).
2. **Changed notes reach a continued Claude conversation** (`server/providers/claude.js`). With the condition Codex already used (`refreshSystem` when the notes block hash differs from `noteHashes`), the prompt starts with `Your role instructions have changed. Follow the ones below instead of the ones you got earlier.` and the whole current instructions in the same tag, with `Don't use the role notes you got earlier anymore.` inside when the notes are now empty; the head follows. 0013 is revised.
3. **A continuing meeting turn gets only what is new** (`ctx.meetSeen`, `#meetingPrompt` `heard`). For each participant whose own conversation already holds a turn of this meeting, the meeting remembers how many of the "said so far" entries that conversation has received. Its next turn, verdict or conclusion gets the entries after that, without its own words, grouped by round. A turn in a new conversation (a goal's meeting, a first turn, a takeover by another AI, a failed resume, an AI that can't resume) gets everything as before. When nothing is new, the line explaining the markers is left out.
4. **No skill bodies in meeting talk turns that run in a new conversation** (option B above): every opinion, turn, verdict and conclusion of a goal's meetings and of a long session's normal meetings (item 5). Research during a meeting keeps them. A normal meeting in a shorter session keeps them too, because its turns open or continue the participant's session conversation, which later work continues (see Options). Each of the two system instructions is fixed per agent. Talk turns in a new conversation also don't get the CLAUDE.md line, as in 0017.
5. **Meetings in a long session run once per turn** (`#longTalk`, `ctx.meetFresh`, `once.fresh`, `msg.freshMeet`). Outside goal mode, when the session's earlier conversation that can be handed on (`#talkList`, the same list `#fullTalk` uses) has more messages than `historyTurns` × 2 (16 by default), every turn of the meeting, research included, runs once in a new conversation: no conversation id is read or saved. Each turn gets the request in full, the earlier stage results as now, what was said in the meeting, and the "picked parts of the earlier conversation" block (`#meetPicks`, `pickEarlier` over that list with `MEET_PICKS`: 3,000 characters in all, 600 per message; an agent taking over a session gets up to 32,000 characters of recent messages, 4,000 of picked parts (20,000 for an AI without tools) and the whole-conversation file). There is no recent conversation, no whole-conversation file, no handoff note, no project memory and no missed conversation. Extra instructions are taken as before; one that can't be injected live goes into the same turn's conversation afterwards. The turns are marked `once` and `freshMeet`; unlike a goal's meeting turns they are handed on to later conversations like other answers. The next stage gets the conclusion as before. With `historyTurns` 0, any session with an earlier message counts as long and no earlier messages are picked.

This adds `freshMeet` to messages. Older versions ignore it and would leave such a meeting's turns out of handed-on conversations, as they do for a goal's meeting turns.

## Measurement

The same scenes, the same script and the same counting before and after this change (test CLIs with answer lengths padded to realistic sizes). Characters, not tokens: a Korean character is often about one token and English about four characters per token, so the two don't scale together. "Sent" is what the app sends (system instructions of a new conversation plus the prompt). "Read again" is the earlier text of a continued CLI conversation that the call reads again. "Variants" adds up, per agent, the different system instructions its new conversations got.

| Scene | Calls (new/continued) | Sent | Read again | Sent + read again | Variants (agents) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Three-stage team, first request | 3 (3/0) → 3 (3/0) | 32,848 → 32,851 (+0.0%) | 0 → 0 | 32,848 → 32,851 | 3 → 3 (3) |
| Same team, second request | 3 (0/3) → 3 (0/3) | 16,202 → 16,202 | 41,884 → 41,887 | 58,086 → 58,089 (+0.0%) | none new |
| Meeting in a fresh session (3 participants, 3 rounds, then a stage) | 10 (4/6) → 10 (4/6) | 43,333 → 35,015 (−19.2%) | 56,943 → 55,399 (−2.7%) | 100,276 → 90,414 (−9.8%) | 4 → 4 (4) |
| The same meeting in a session with 34 earlier messages | 10 (4/6) → 10 (10/0) | 116,039 → 50,342 (−56.6%) | 166,032 → 0 | 282,071 → 50,342 (−82.2%) | 4 → 4 (4) |
| Full goal run (research, meeting, 4 task cards, one return) | 21 (20/1) → 21 (20/1) | 263,061 → 222,461 (−15.4%) | 11,767 → 11,768 | 274,828 → 234,229 (−14.8%) | 9 → 6 (5) |

- The team scenes did not get smaller: the per-call lines moved but are still sent (3 characters more in all, one blank line per call). Their gain is that each agent's system instructions are now the same text on every call, which the cache can reuse; how much that saves in real use wasn't measured.
- In the goal run the 6 variants are one per agent plus the research agent's second, skill-free instructions for its meeting turns, by design; before, the research agent had 3 and the analyst 2 inside the same kind of call. The skill bodies sent in new conversations went from 144,802 to 103,430 characters.
- In the fresh-session meeting the gain is the turns that no longer repeat what their conversation holds (8,318 characters fewer sent); the research agent there still gets its skills (item 4).
- In the long-session meeting nothing is read again: each meeting turn got 703 characters of picked earlier messages, where before the continuing turns read back 19,314 to 42,633 characters of their own conversation (which had started with the recent conversation, picked parts and the file path).
- Under the cache model the goal run's characters written fresh went from 176,814 to 136,041 (−23.1%) and the long-session meeting's from 71,134 to 40,098 (−43.6%). A model, not cache numbers from a real CLI.
- The scenes not shown (a session of 17 requests to one agent, an agent with skills, role notes and project memory) moved by at most 89 characters sent and 355 read again: one prompt had or lacked the "nothing found in project memory" line depending on when the memory had indexed an earlier answer, not because of this change.

## Not done

- Giving skills by name and path only, and letting the CLI read them, is left for later: whether a CLI reads a skill it is only pointed at was not checked with a real CLI.
- Writing the research note to a file and sending a path and a summary, sending only the latest earlier stage result in full, fewer recent messages (`historyTurns`), a light mode for one-off calls, not sending again what a continued card conversation already holds, and a usage screen by kind of call are left for later.
- Real cache hits were not measured: the numbers are characters from the test CLIs, and the cache model above is a model.

## Consequences

- Risk: lines that were system instructions are now in the user's message: the read-only sentence of a goal's research and meeting calls, the CLAUDE.md line, the "how to ask" lines and the role notes' reference block. A model may weigh them less. What a read-only call can do is still held by the CLI flags (0017), and the reference block was reference material already. A read-only call now sees the sentence for the agent's saved permission in its instructions and "this turn is read-only" in its message.
- Risk: meeting participants in a long session know less of the session than before (no recent conversation, file, handoff note or memory; picked parts only). A meeting that depends on something said in the middle of a long session can miss it. The threshold is a count of messages, so one more message can switch a session to this behaviour.
- Risk: a continuing turn relies on its CLI conversation still holding the earlier turns. A compacted conversation may only hold a summary of them; they are not sent again.
- A notes change in a continued Claude conversation sends the whole instructions again once, skill bodies included (up to 20,000 characters per skill), as Codex already did. After a Claude conversation is compacted it switches to the current, fixed instructions, and the reference block from its first message may be summarised away.
- Tests that look at the body of prompts strip the head with `promptBody` (`test/helpers.js`); `test/prompt-assembly.test.js` checks the head itself. The demo provider and the test Claude find the research headings by line, since the head can come first.
- To undo: put the `head` lines back into the `#system` text and stop prepending them in `#runAgent`; drop the `talk` argument for skills; drop `refreshSystem` handling in `claude.js`; pass `null` as `heard` in `#prompt`; set `ctx.meetFresh` to false. `freshMeet` on saved messages can stay.
