# Changelog

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Goal mode, sixth part (decision 0019): a call estimate and a wait for usage limits. Before you confirm, the goal card shows "about a to b calls (including the calls already used to set the goal and up to one automatic return) · cap 40"; it changes at once when you tick research or the meeting, switch between task cards and the team's stages, or keep a review stage (and is redrawn from the current team when a confirm is refused because the team changed), says it is a rough figure and not a forecast, says that re-asks, retries and takeovers cost more, and says it will ask at the cap when the upper end passes 40. After the goal ends (passed, accepted or stopped) the card shows the actual number of calls next to the estimate (older goals show only the actual number). The card also lists, for the AIs the team members are set to, the usage windows the app has already received from earlier runs (named as in the usage panel: 5-hour, weekly and so on) with the reset time and how old the record is (minutes, hours or days; over a day it is marked as an old record); windows that already reset or have no percentage are left out, the line is hidden when there is nothing to show, and nothing is read anew or estimated. When a work stage (a team stage or a task card step) or the criteria check stops at a usage limit and no other AI can take over, the goal now waits instead of showing the error wait (or ending the check without a verdict). The choices: **Continue after HH:MM (automatic)** (only when the reset time is known from a full window, 100% or rejected, or from the error message), **Retry now**, **Skip** for a work stage or **Accept** for the check (the exits the earlier waits had: a skipped task card is recorded as a gap, an accepted check ends the goal as accepted without a verdict), and **Drop it**. Automatic continue starts the same step 45 seconds after the reset time, waits instead if you paused, can be used three times per goal, and the other choices still work while it waits. Before any retry the call cap is checked ("20 more?" first when it is short). A retry runs only the people or cards that hit the limit and keeps finished answers (a task card retried after a limit isn't counted as another start); the check runs the same round again and the answer cut by the limit is marked as such. If the app closes during the wait, the timer is gone: the cut notice shows when the limit was due to reset, and **Continue** shows the same question again without an AI call (saying the reset time passed when it has). Not covered: the percentages are only what the app already received; the limit messages of the real CLIs weren't checked in this change; the estimate's accuracy isn't measured beyond the fake CLI tests; a usage limit during the research, the pre-work meeting, the plan, a team meeting stage or the goal turn keeps its earlier handling; the automatic continue timer doesn't survive closing the app.
- Goal mode, fifth part (decision 0018): task cards. The goal card has a "How to work" part: **Split into task cards** (the default when the team can) or **Follow the team's stages**. With cards, the lead writes a plan once (read-only, one call): 2 to 6 cards, each with an owner from the team list, a kind (research, write, build, check), what it waits for, the files it owns, what it must not touch and when it is done. Office Crew numbers the cards, checks the owners against the team (asks once more for unknown names, then gives the card to a matching member and says so), and orders the cards into steps itself: a card waits for another only when it needs that card's result; one card per person per step; cards that list overlapping files run in different steps; a build or write card that lists no files runs alone. A plan card in the chat shows the cards and "runs in n steps"; you don't have to confirm it. Each owner gets the confirmed goal, their card and the results of the cards it waits for (their closing notes first), all marked as reference, and ends with two short lines, "Changed files" and "For the next card" (asked for once more if missing; missing twice, the card is recorded as a gap and isn't counted as done). The stage strip shows "Plan → Work 1/n …" (hover a step for its cards), and the progress tab lists the cards with their status; a waiting card isn't counted as working and says what it waits for. A card can start at most 3 times (retries and returns count) before you are asked, a goal asks before starting more than 20 cards in an hour, and an error retry re-runs only the cards that failed. A goal cut while planning plans again; once the plan exists it is kept, and continuing starts only the cards that were cut. Not covered: teams with an approval gate or a meeting stage can't use cards; you can't edit the plan; the plan isn't changed during the run; a card can't call a meeting or ask the lead; nothing stops an agent from writing outside the files on its card; how real CLIs follow the plan block isn't measured (tests use fake CLIs).
- Goal mode, card and check (decisions 0014 and 0015 revised): each criterion can say how to check it and the goal can list what must not change; both are shown and editable on the goal card, compared when you confirm, and given to every work stage, card and the criteria check. Every goal work stage (also with the team's stages) can end with a "blocked" block instead of forcing a criterion it can't meet; the run then stops before the check and asks you with the reason (accept, which leaves out only the blocked work and goes on to the check with the rest, recording a blocked card as a gap; run only the blocked work once more; or stop). The reviewer now marks a criterion as failed even for a small problem, but only with evidence (a fail without evidence counts as not checked), writes problems outside the criteria as follow-ups that are shown but don't block, and reports a broken "must not change" item, which makes the goal ask you even if every criterion passed. When the work folder is a git repository the check also gets the list of changed files, and changed test files get a line in the progress tab. The goal card says whether the reviewer can run commands and tests or only read files. A second return, or the same criterion failing twice in a row, asks the agent to write down the assumption all attempts shared and check it before fixing. Not covered: structured output options of the CLIs aren't used; the blocks are read from text.
- Goal mode, fourth part (decision 0017): the team researches and meets before it works. The goal card has a new "Research and meet first" part with two boxes, Research and Meeting, both on by default; when the lead thinks the goal is small it can suggest starting right away, and then both start off (you can still turn them on). A box is locked, with the reason, when the team can't do that step: no agent with a research role, fewer than two people who can join the meeting (the lead and team members who aren't builders or reviewers, up to 5), or the lead is no longer in the team. The card also shows an estimate of the extra AI calls (asking again for a block or an empty answer, or a retry, costs more). Starting is refused, and the card is shown again, when the lead or the research agents or meeting participants changed since the card was drawn. With Research on, after you confirm: one research agent takes a quick broad look, the lead writes 2 to 6 research questions, each question is looked up by a research agent (several agents at the same time), and a research note card collects the answers in the chat (how many questions couldn't be checked, the start of each answer, "Show all" to jump to the full answer, marked as written by an agent). A question that fails, is skipped or comes back empty is marked "not checked" and the others go on; if the lead's questions can't be read, one general question is researched instead. With Meeting on, the lead and the participants hold a two-round "Pre-work meeting" over the research note before the team's stages. Every turn in these steps starts a new AI conversation and doesn't touch the agents' ongoing conversations, their answers don't go into project memory, and they aren't handed to agents as earlier conversation (the previous or missed conversation, picked parts, the whole-conversation file), also after a cut goal is continued. The work stages get the research note and the meeting's conclusion as reference material marked as not confirmed by you; the criteria check doesn't get them; only the goal and criteria you confirmed are instructions. The stage strip shows the new steps ("Research ×3"), the progress tab shows a line for each step and the note's summary, and the steps count toward the goal's call limit (each step is checked before it starts). Pause takes effect before each research question too, and Skip without choosing an agent skips the questions running at that moment. A goal cut off during these steps continues from that step: finished steps and finished research questions don't run again, and a meeting starts again from round 1. Messages you send meanwhile are kept as extra instructions for the work stages. Not covered: the meeting can't ask you anything yet, sources in research answers aren't checked by the app, whether a read-only research agent can search the web depends on its CLI, "don't change files" in these steps is only an instruction (each agent runs with its own permission setting), Skip without choosing an agent doesn't skip research questions that haven't started yet, the call cap check before a meeting doesn't count research during the meeting, retries or takeovers, and how real AIs follow the research-question format is not measured yet.
- Goal mode, research and meetings tightened (decision 0017, 2026-11-22): the scan, the research questions, each research question and every turn of a goal's meetings (the pre-work meeting and a team's own meeting stage, research during them included) run read-only for that call only, also after a takeover to another AI, whatever the agent's own permission is. Its saved setting doesn't change, and the work stages and the criteria check run with the agent's own permission. Claude, Codex, Gemini CLI and ACP agents get their read-only mode; for Hermes the research is only told not to change files, and API models and models on this PC have no tools; the research note card names both kinds of agents. Each research question now lists the questions the other branches cover, so the answers don't overlap, and asks for one fact per line with its source and whether it was checked. The research note counts lines that look like instructions (after removing invisible characters, and only lines that read as an order, not ones that merely mention such a thing) and list lines without a source (an address, `file:line` or `path/file.ext`), on the card and at the head of the note; an answer with an instruction-like line gets a warning on the card, goes to the pre-work meeting with a warning, and reaches the work stages without those lines, with one line saying how many were left out. A research result during a goal's meeting gets the same counts and marks. The demo's research answers now give `https://example.test/…` sources. In a goal's meetings (the pre-work meeting and a team's own meeting stage) the opinion ends with a position line, in the turns everyone is told to write first what they don't agree with and what made them change their position, a participant's own earlier words are shown apart from the others', and the opposing side is told to rebut the strongest claim with evidence. When the turn rounds would cost more than one call (three or more participants, or more than two rounds), the chair judges once after round 1 and, when everyone, the opposing side included, takes the same position, the turn rounds are skipped (one line in the progress tab either way); the card's estimate says how many calls that saves. Meetings outside goal mode are unchanged. Not covered: what each CLI's read-only mode lets through (in particular web search and web fetch) was not run with a real CLI; an ACP agent that runs a tool without asking isn't stopped; the two counts are patterns: an instruction written as a description isn't counted, and a source written in words or a bare file name counts as no source; when the positions differ, the judgement after round 1 costs one more call; the app doesn't count who changed position; a goal started in an older version keeps that version's behavior for the steps it already ran, and an older version shows the new research note card without its counts and marks.
- Goal mode, third part (decision 0016): a goal cut off by closing the app can be continued from where it stopped, also after closing the app normally. While a goal runs, Office Crew saves its place at once: the stage, which agents have their AI running, which answers of the stage are already finished, and the question it waits at. After the app opens again nothing starts by itself; the cut notice (on the goal card, in the progress tab and above the message box) has a **Continue** button next to **Discard** and says where the goal starts again. Continuing starts a new run at that point: earlier stages don't run again, and their latest finished answers are gathered from the conversation and passed on as before. In the cut stage, agents that had already finished keep their answers and don't run again; only the agents whose AI was cut start a new conversation, and they are told, in their first call only, to look at the work folder first and continue from what is already there. A meeting cut in the middle runs again from round 1; since the fourth part (decision 0017) every meeting turn in a goal runs in a new conversation, so the participants keep their saved conversations and the meeting turns get no "[Continuing]" line. A goal cut during a return continues that return (with the failed criteria), one cut during the criteria check runs the check again, and one cut while asking you (an approval, an error, a verdict, unused extra instructions or the call cap) shows the same question again without any AI call. Calls, returns and extra instructions carry on from the saved values. Continuing is refused with the reason when something changed since the goal started: the team's stages (names, agents, approval steps, meetings and their settings, instructions), an agent's kind of role, or the session's work folder; also when another run is going, or when the team now mixes a model on this PC with cloud AIs. The goal stays cut and can still be discarded. An agent's AI, model or permission is not compared. A session's run list keeps the runs of a cut goal when it is trimmed to the last 30. Not covered: half-done work of the cut AI (a half-written file) is left for the agent to judge; the turns a meeting had before the cut are not reused; the usage of the cut call isn't counted; a goal cut while the lead was drafting the card, or cut before this version, can only be discarded; in a narrow window the box above the message field can be hidden behind the side panel; how real CLIs pick up the work is not measured (fake CLIs only).
- Goal mode, second part (decision 0015): a fresh reviewer checks each confirmed criterion. The goal card now says which team member will judge the criteria (the first agent of the team's review stages, the stages where every agent has a review role, who isn't the lead, even if that agent also works in the last stage; else the first member who is neither the lead nor in the last work stage; never someone outside the team) and lists the team's review stages that the check replaces (review stages without an approval step that aren't meetings), each with a **Keep** box and the start of its instructions. If the reviewer or those stages changed after the card was drafted (also when the team's stages were reordered), starting is refused and the card is redrawn. After the team's stages a "Criteria check" stage runs: the reviewer starts a new conversation that the session doesn't keep (its conversation, usage baseline and role-note record for the session are untouched, it gets no earlier conversation or project memory, can't open a question box, and its answer isn't stored in project memory), with the confirmed goal, the numbered criteria, the latest answer of each work stage as reference (first 1,500 characters) and a one-time value. Its verdict counts only as one `oc-review` block at the very end with that value and no other marked block; unknown criteria become "not checked", a pass without evidence becomes "not checked", and the same criterion twice keeps the worse verdict. A broken verdict is asked for once more; after that, or if the reviewer fails, every criterion is "not checked". All passed: the goal is done. A failed criterion sends the last work stage back once, with the failed criteria, the reviewer's evidence and that stage's previous result as marked reference (a meeting is redone by the lead alone with its conclusion; an approval stage asks for approval again), then the check runs again; stages after it don't run again, so their old answers are left out of that check. Anything left after that, or only "not checked", or extra instructions that came in after the last stage started: a box in the chat asks to accept (the goal ends "accepted" with the verdicts kept), go once more (exactly one more return), or stop. An instruction sent from an agent's panel to the agent doing the check is not put into the check; it becomes an extra instruction and is asked about after the verdict. Each goal counts its AI calls; before a stage would go over 40 it asks for 20 more or stop. The review answer shows a table of the verdicts marked "written by an agent · not confirmed by you" instead of the raw block; the Progress tab shows each criterion's verdict, the reviewer and "used / cap" calls; the sidebar's waiting list and notices name these waits. The demo team does the check too, at no usage. Costs at least one more AI call per goal (two when the verdict has to be asked again), plus the redone stage and another check on a return. Not covered: the reviewer runs with its own permission setting, so "don't change files" is only an instruction (raise that agent's permission if it should run tests); a reviewer on the same AI as the workers only gets the existing notice; the call cap is checked before a stage starts, against the stage's number of agents (a meeting stage against all its turns since decision 0017), so retries and takeovers inside a stage and research during a meeting can go over it, and tokens or subscription percentage aren't capped; a team without another member to review ends without a verdict and asks you; confirmed criteria still can't be changed; only the last work stage is redone; how reliably real CLIs write the verdict block and how honestly they judge is not measured (fake CLIs only).
- Goal mode, first part (decision 0014): a "To the goal" switch next to the message box, per session. With it on, a request (without `@name`) first goes to the team's lead alone, who turns it into a goal card in the chat: the goal in one line, 2 to 7 acceptance criteria that can be checked, what is out of scope, and what only you can decide. Nothing else runs until you press "Start as is", "Edit and start" (edit each list one item per line) or "Drop it". The app starts only if the card you saw is exactly the stored draft (otherwise it redraws the card and asks again), and then runs the team's stage list once, every stage included (review stages and approval steps too), with the confirmed goal and numbered criteria at the start of each stage's own instructions (what the app already passed before a stage's instructions, such as the earlier conversation for an agent starting fresh, still comes first). While the lead drafts the card, what you send goes to the lead whichever send mode you picked. Messages you send while the goal runs (up to 50) become extra instructions instead of new runs: they go into every stage that starts after them, and into a stage that runs again when you reject it or press Retry; with the second part, ones that come after the last stage started are asked about after the criteria check. They go into project memory when the goal run ends, marked as seen only by models on this PC when only those received them. "Send as is" on a hire card also starts a goal turn in a goal-mode session (refused while a goal is open). If goal mode turns off because you picked a team it can't use, a message says why. A new "Progress" tab in the right panel shows the goal, the criteria and a line for each step; click a line to jump to its message. If the lead's answer has no readable card it is asked once more, and then the card is made from your request with empty criteria for you to fill in; a draft with common injection patterns gets a warning on the card. That check, shared with role notes, now also catches tags that imitate the one the app wraps an agent's instructions in (such as `<role instructions>`), so role notes now refuse such text from agents, outside sources and chat picks too; angle brackets in the confirmed goal and in extra instructions become full-width in the prompt. Costs one AI call before the work starts (two when the card has to be asked for again). Judging the criteria, one automatic return and the call cap came with the second part (the entry above). Not covered yet: confirmed criteria can't be changed. Pausing while the lead drafts the card does nothing, an extra instruction sent by mistake can't be taken back, and extra-instruction messages stay out of the earlier conversation passed to agents even after the goal ended. A team of cloud AIs with an automatic-routing agent is allowed even though that agent can end up on a model on this PC. When you confirm, sentences the lead wrote become instructions, so read the card first; the warning catches common injection patterns only. The goal turn always starts a new conversation for the lead, and the lead's earlier conversation is put back afterwards. Goal mode can't be used in a session without a team, in scheduled runs or board cards, or with a team that mixes models on this PC with cloud AIs or automatic routing. The demo team answers the goal turn too, so the flow can be tried at no usage. Tested with fake CLIs only; how well real CLIs follow the card format is not measured.
- Role notes (decision 0013): each agent keeps what you teach it. The agent editor has a new "Role notes" part with the role's tasks (up to 8, each with when it counts as done and how to check), short items of up to 400 characters (method, pitfall, criterion or tool use) with their source, and the last 10 changes, each with an Undo button; a "Go to role notes" button at the top of the editor jumps there. Items you write count at once; items from an agent or an outside source come in as drafts, and only after you accept them (one by one or "Accept all drafts") do they go into that agent's next runs. Nothing is deleted: Archive and Discard keep the item out of runs, and Restore on an archived item brings it back (your own items as accepted, items from an agent or an outside source as drafts to accept again). An item you add can be marked "Only for models on this PC". The editor's Save also saves changed tasks and stays open if that fails, closing the editor with notes text you haven't saved asks first, and Esc in the task fields and the add box doesn't close it. In the chat, select text in an agent's answer, right-click and choose "Add to role notes" to add it to that agent's notes as your own item (the first 400 characters, shown as "Picked from the chat"). It counts at once, but it is first checked for common injection patterns and refused with a hint to write it in the editor yourself, and when a model on this PC wrote that answer the item goes only to models on this PC (the app looks at the message itself, not at what the screen says). Each run puts the role's tasks and the accepted items before the agent's skills in its instructions, up to 2,000 characters counting each item's printed source address (cut to 120 characters): all of them, newest first, when they fit, otherwise the ones that share the most words with the request. Your items go in as rules, and items from agents or outside sources inside markers as reference material it must not take as instructions; angle brackets in notes become full-width there, so a note can't close the tag the instructions are wrapped in. A continued Codex conversation gets the instructions again, after a line saying they changed, when the notes changed since it last got them, with a line not to use the earlier notes when they were all removed; Claude gets the instructions on every call and the other AIs start a new conversation on every run. Drafts show as a "Drafts n" chip on the Agents page and on the agent's card in the session's right panel; clicking it opens the notes. For the API, adding an item needs a source, `localOnly` must be true or false, and an item's status can only move along a fixed table (see decision 0013). Not covered: agents do not yet write or look up notes by themselves (drafts can only come through the API for now), the helped and harmed counts stay at 0, notes are not exported with a plugin or synced with another PC, the check on outside and picked text catches common injection patterns only and can also refuse a correct rule that uses those words (write it in the editor then), an agent that can run commands can get around these rules through the local API or the notes files (the same as the scope in SECURITY.md), a continued conversation still has the old notes in it and is only told to stop using them, a change to the agent's own instructions alone still doesn't reach a continued Codex conversation, there is no limit on the number of items per agent, invisible characters such as ZWNJ are removed from items you write, an item taken from a local model's answer goes only to local models but a local model can still repeat it in an answer that a later cloud stage reads, Undo covers only the last 10 changes and refuses when the same item changed after that, a notes file that can't be read makes the run go without notes (with a notice) and is left as it is, whether notes improve results has not been measured, nothing was run with real CLIs, and the screen changes were checked with a throwaway script that builds them in Node; looked at in a real browser: the jump button, Esc and the close question in a task field, Save also saving the tasks, opening the notes from the draft chip, Discard, Restore and Accept, the "Only for models on this PC" box (it clears after each add), picking text in the chat, and the instructions a fake Claude run received; not looked at: the dark theme, a narrow window, the Undo buttons, editing an item, and the continued Codex conversation (tests only).
- Wait for a limit that resets soon (Routing → Automatic handoff, off by default): a number of minutes, 0 to 30. When an agent with a chosen AI is about to start and that AI is resting or near its usage limit, and the limit record says the full window resets within that many minutes, the run waits for the reset and then runs on the same AI instead of handing the work to another AI. After that wait, the 30 minutes the app otherwise keeps avoiding an AI after a rejection was seen do not hold the run back, because the window that was full has reset. While it waits the agent shows "Paused" with the minutes left (not "waiting", which means it needs you), and the answer gets a notice that it waited. Stop ends the wait and the agent does not run; "End meeting" ends it and skips that turn; a Skip button on the agent's card in the right panel ends it and skips only that agent; Pause does not end it, so the agent still runs on the same AI after the wait and the run pauses at the next stage boundary as usual. An instruction you send to the waiting agent (mention it and choose "Interrupt now") goes into its prompt when it starts, instead of to the next stage. A number outside 0 to 30 is saved as the nearest allowed value, and the field shows the saved value. Other agents of the same stage keep working while one waits. Not covered: it applies only when a run starts, not to a limit hit in the middle of a run (that still hands over at once); only to an AI whose limit record gives the reset time of the full window (when the app only guesses 30 minutes, or the time came from the error text, it hands over at once); not to agents on automatic routing, not to scheduled runs, and not when the AI has also used up today's budget share; if the AI is still blocked after the wait, the work goes to another AI as before; the chat shows no message while an agent waits, only the right panel and the office do; the agent's detail panel shows no Skip button while it waits, and a message typed there is queued as a new request instead of going into the waiting run.
- Research during a meeting (decision 0011): from the second round, a participant that does not know a fact that could change the conclusion is told not to guess but to end its turn with a question in a marked block. The team's research agent (the first agent with a research role, in stage order; never the chair) then looks the question up once with its tools and answers in a few lines with a source for each fact, and the following turns, verdicts and the conclusion see that answer as reference material. In the chat the turn shows "🔎 Needs checking" with the question and a status chip (Researching, Researched, No one to research, Research limit reached, Not checked), the block itself is hidden, and the research answer is labeled Research r/n with whose question it answers. The run bar shows Research r/n while it runs. A message you send while a research runs, without picking an agent, is not given to the researcher but to the following turns, like a message sent between turns; a message you send to the researcher by picking it still goes to it. The demo agents show it too: the opposer asks one question in round 2. Not covered: a team with nobody to do the research (no research-role agent other than the chair) cannot research: its turns are not told about the question block, and a question that comes anyway is marked and the next turns are told it was not checked; each researched question is one more AI call (three participants, two rounds and one question make seven calls); at most three questions are researched per meeting (a retry of the stage counts from three again), and a later question is not researched: the next turn is told it was not checked, with the question, and no AI call is made; the research agent continues the session's CLI conversation like a participant, so research in a long session is expensive; the research agent is told to only read and search what it needs, and not to edit files, install or delete anything or run commands that change state, but its permission level is not lowered, so for an agent with edit or run permission nothing but that instruction stops it; a research that fails, is skipped or is stopped with "End meeting" leaves "not checked" and the meeting goes on, while stopping the run stops the meeting too; the app does not check that the sources exist or say what the answer claims; a question block that can't be read is left as text with a notice; the question block stays in the message text, so with project memory on it can be stored in memory; checked with fake CLIs and the demo agents only, not with real CLIs.

- Agents can ask you with choices: when an agent cannot decide something by itself, a question box opens right above the message box with 1 to 4 questions, one at a time, and 2 to 6 numbered choices each. Click a choice or press its number key to answer at once (several can be picked where the question allows it), or type your own answer. You can collapse the box and answer later. When you answer, the same agent continues in the same reply, and the question and your answer stay in the conversation. "Skip" lets the agent pick an assumption and continue. File paths show as names only and commands as the program name only; the end of the AI's latest answer is shown too, with common token shapes masked, so take care when sharing your screen. A session shows as idle after 90 seconds without a log line, or 30 minutes when its last line started a tool, such as a long build. The log format belongs to each CLI and can change between versions; unknown lines are skipped. Up to 12 recent sessions are shown, and the page says how many are hidden. Added ChatGPT accounts and Codex sessions started more than two days ago aren't watched.
- Daily budget (off by default, on the Routing board): keeps a weekly limit from going in a day or two. You set a daily share of the weekly window (13% by default). Once a quarter of the share is used and today is going faster than the day, a run's effort is lowered one or two steps (never raised, never below low; the saved agent doesn't change). An AI that used its share hands off to another AI until midnight; with no other AI available, or with auto-handoff turned off, work continues at low effort. A review keeps its cross-check first: it may still use an AI that's over budget, at low effort, rather than the AI that built the result. An AI taking over after a usage limit follows the same pace. Today's spend counts from the first weekly reading the app sees today (usage while the app was closed isn't counted), and only AIs that report weekly usage are affected. "Use again" on an AI that's over its share lifts it for the rest of the day. A scheduled run is stricter: if no AI within budget can take it, that time is skipped instead of running at low effort ("Run now" still runs).
- Desktop notifications (off by default, Settings → Storage & performance): when the window is in the background, a notification tells you about approvals, decisions, finished work and failures. Clicking it opens the session. Permission belongs to each browser, so the setting says when this one hasn't allowed it. Work that ends while the page is disconnected doesn't notify.
- Stalled run watch: if a CLI gives no output for 30 minutes (Settings → Defaults, 0 turns it off), it's stopped and goes to the usual error step. A stuck scheduled run ends by itself instead, so later runs aren't held up. While a tool is running it waits up to four times as long, since builds and tests can be quiet. Hermes isn't watched, because it gives its whole result at the end.
- Continuing a conversation now carries what you missed: when an agent picks its conversation back up, it also gets what was said with other agents since its last answer, limited by the recent-conversation setting and marked as reference rather than instructions. A conversation with a local model isn't passed to a cloud AI this way, or when recent conversation is added to a new or handed-over run.
- Leftover CLI cleanup: running CLIs are recorded in `running-cli` in the data folder. If the app was killed or the power went out, the next start stops CLIs it left behind, only when both the start time and the name match (so a reused process ID never stops another program), and leaves the record of another running copy of the app alone.
- Schedules: on the days and at the time you pick, a team (or a single agent) gets the same instruction and starts working on its own, in a new session each time or continuing one session. Times use this PC's clock and only run while the app is open. A time missed while the app was closed is skipped by default, or run once when the app opens if you turn that on. If the previous run hasn't finished, that time is skipped. Approval steps wait for you as usual. Run now, turn on or off, and the last result are on each card.

### Changed
- Model and effort names match each CLI: Claude models show their version the way Claude Code does (Opus 5.5, Fable 5.1, Sonnet 5.5, Haiku 5.5), with "(my default)" on the model from your Claude Code settings. The version an alias resolved to in a real run is remembered across restarts. Codex lists every model the Codex app shows, previous generations included, named as the Codex app does (GPT-6.1 Sol). Effort levels are Low, Medium, High, Extra high, Max (and Ultra where a Codex model supports it), with "(my default)" for the level in your CLI settings and "(recommended)" or "(default)" for the model's own. "Default (follow CLI settings)" is at the top of the model list, and Max on Claude warns that it uses much more.
- Auto-routing no longer picks previous-generation Codex models.
- Read-only agents no longer load hooks from the work folder, so asking an agent to only read a downloaded repository can't run that repository's commands. Claude Code runs them with `--setting-sources user` (settings and hooks from the work folder aren't loaded, your own user settings still are, and they're told to read project rule files such as `CLAUDE.md` and `AGENTS.md` themselves). Codex runs read-only and default-permission agents with `features.hooks=false` (your own Codex hooks are off too in those runs). Claude Code at other permission levels, and Codex at Edit or higher, work as before.
- Claude Code runs get a permission rule that refuses reading the sign-in files of Claude Code, Codex (added accounts and a custom `CODEX_HOME` included), Hermes and Gemini CLI, plus this app's settings (where API keys live) and access token. This covers Claude's file-reading tools; an agent allowed to run commands can still reach them through the shell.
- Picking a risky work folder is refused: a drive's top level, your whole home folder (or above it) and the app's data folder (or a folder containing it). Sessions already using such a folder, such as an imported one, keep working.
- Team runs: earlier stages' results are passed inside markers that change every time, with a note not to follow anything in them that tries to widen permissions or change the request. This lowers the risk of text an earlier stage read (a web page, a repository's documents) acting as an instruction later; it doesn't remove it.
- Crew Village is now the README's demo and office screenshots, with new pictures for schedules, skills, skins and a customized village.
- `오피스크루 실행.cmd` no longer waits for a key press when Office Crew is already running; it pauses only on an error.

### Fixed
- Skills: descriptions written over several lines in `SKILL.md` (`description: >` or `|`) were read as a single `>` or `|`, and a long header could leave the description empty. Multi-line and quoted values are now read in full.
- The page is marked as not to be translated, so browser translation extensions no longer add translations next to model names and labels (for example "Gemini" turned into a word meaning "twins").
- Settings → AI accounts: when Hermes is not signed in, the provider from its config (for example Nous Portal) no longer shows as a signed-in account. The card says "Login required" and names the configured provider in the note instead.

## [1.1.0] - 2026-11-03

### Added
- Needs you: the top of the sidebar collects sessions that need you (approvals, decisions after an error, paused runs, failures and results you haven't opened yet). Results and failures leave the list once you open them; approvals, decisions and paused runs stay until they're handled.
- Handoff note: when another AI takes over after a usage limit, it also gets a note the server pulled from the tool log: tools used (with failed and unfinished counts), files changed (relative to the work folder) and the last three commands, plus an instruction to check the current state before continuing. Commands keep only the program, well-known subcommands and plain file names (`npm test`, `git commit …`); the other arguments, where tokens and passwords usually sit, are left out. Names, paths and commands are quoted and marked as values, not instructions.
- Evidence-based review: review agents are told to judge only by evidence they checked themselves and to list anything they couldn't check. A review done by the same AI that built the result, including one that took the review over after a usage limit, is flagged as weaker than a cross-check. The same AI means the same CLI (Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI, Hermes), or the same API profile and model, ACP agent or local model.

### Changed
- Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and Hermes now rest until the reset time stated in the limit error when their usage data has no full window, instead of a flat 30 minutes. Recognized forms: `Retry-After`, "try again in …", "try again at …", "resets 3pm" and an epoch suffix; a time zone after the time (UTC, an offset such as UTC+9, or a name such as `(America/Los_Angeles)`) is honored. A time that passed less than two hours ago falls back to 30 minutes, and waits shorter than a minute become a minute.
- Auto-routed review agents avoid the AIs that built the result (every earlier build stage, or the previous stage when there was no build). Before, only the nearest stage with output was avoided.
- Environment variables and headers of connectors, and environment variables of ACP agents: names that contain `AUTH` or `COOKIE` but end in a setting word, such as `LOGIN`, `LABEL`, `GUARD`, `MODEL`, `PKCE` or `ONLY`, are shown as normal values instead of being masked.

### Fixed
- ACP file requests that used a Windows short (8.3) path for a file inside the work folder were refused. Other paths outside the work folder are still refused before the file system is touched, except on Windows when the path or the work folder contains `~` (a possible short name) and the path is on the same drive; those are resolved first and then refused if they really are outside.

## [1.0.0] - 2026-11-02

First public release.

### Added

#### Providers
- Runs your own installed and signed-in official CLIs: Claude Code, OpenAI Codex CLI, Gemini CLI (API key) and Nous Hermes Agent.
- Billing per AI for Claude Code and Codex: subscription sign-in or an official API key (`ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` / `CODEX_API_KEY`). Subscription mode strips those variables from the CLI environment so a stray key never bills you by surprise.
- ACP (Agent Client Protocol) agents: presets for Gemini CLI `--acp`, Hermes `acp`, OpenCode, Goose and Qwen Code, or any custom ACP command. Permission requests follow the agent's permission level; file reads and writes the agent asks Office Crew to do stay inside the work folder (reads also cover attachments).
- OpenAI-compatible APIs: OpenAI, OpenRouter, Nous Portal, xAI and the Gemini API.
- Local models detected automatically: Ollama, LM Studio, llama.cpp, vLLM and Jan, plus custom server URLs.
- Built-in demo provider with zero usage, for trying the app and for tests.
- Settings → AI accounts: install status, version, signed-in account and supported effort levels per CLI, with a sign-in button that opens the CLI's login in a terminal.

#### Agents and routing
- Per-agent provider, model, effort (low to max, xhigh where supported), permission (read-only to full access), system prompt, skills, MCP connectors and avatar.
- Model dropdowns fed by each CLI's own model catalog, refreshed automatically every 30 minutes and when a CLI update is detected.
- Auto-routing, the default for new agents: picks a model and effort at run time from the AIs you're signed in to, by role (research, organize, analysis, build, review, writing) and profile (economy, balanced, best). Review is cross-checked by a different AI than the builder whenever possible.
- Routing board to edit every agent's routing in one table.
- Automatic handoff: when an AI hits its usage limit, another AI with headroom takes the same role and finishes from where it stopped. AIs that are resting or above a usage threshold (95% by default) are avoided before a run. Toggle, threshold and manual release on the Routing board.

#### Teams and runs
- Team pipelines: stages run in order, agents within a stage run in parallel, and previous outputs are handed off automatically.
- Approval gates (approve, or request changes to rerun the stage with feedback) and an error gate (retry, skip or stop).
- Live intervention: steer a running agent (injected into the running process for Claude), queue messages, pause/resume, stop, and skip a single agent.
- Real-time usage per message, agent and session: estimated tokens while streaming, real counts at turn end, cost where the CLI reports it, and account limits (Claude 5-hour and weekly %, Codex weekly %).

#### Live office
- Isometric SVG office with no game engine. Characters walk to the Research room, Library, Server Room or Meeting Room by tool type, raise a hand for approvals, sweat on errors, carry documents on handoff, and spawn sub-agent helpers.
- Skins: Modern, Cozy and Night, plus custom image skins with a desk and spot editor.
- Quality levels `full`, `lite` and `static` for low-end PCs.

#### Desktop-app experience
- Sessions with search and pinning, image paste (Ctrl+V), drag and drop, `@agent` mentions and `/skill` commands.
- Markdown with copy buttons, resend and edit, export a chat to `.md`, and dark mode.
- Skills discovered automatically from Claude and Codex skill folders, MCP connectors injected per agent, and plugins that bundle agents, teams, skills, connectors and skins into one JSON file (import/export).
- Import and continue Claude Code and Codex sessions without losing context. Claude continues on a fork (`--fork-session`) so the original history is untouched; if resuming fails, the recent conversation is passed along instead.
- UI languages: Korean, English, Simplified Chinese and Japanese.

#### Platform and security
- Zero runtime dependencies (Node.js built-ins only), no build step, UI built with vendored Preact and htm (about 16 KB).
- Launcher opens an Edge/Chrome app window, with `--browser`, `--port`, `--home` and `--no-open` options; Windows launcher script `오피스크루 실행.cmd`.
- Server bound to `127.0.0.1` with `Host`/`Origin` checks and a per-install token; agent output escaped before markdown rendering; API keys masked in the UI.
- Test suite with fake CLIs (zero AI usage) and CI on Windows, Linux and macOS with Node.js 20 and 22.

#### Documentation
- "Using your accounts" guidance in the README (four languages) and in the app: official CLIs only, no access to CLI sign-in tokens, API keys for products, teams and unattended runs.
- Google Antigravity CLI is intentionally not supported, because its personal-account terms don't allow use through third-party tools. Agents saved by pre-release builds that used it switch to auto-routing.
